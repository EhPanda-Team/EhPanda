**The mechanism.** `ContinuedProcessingSession.start` mints
`"\(bundleIdentifier).continued.\(UUID().uuidString)"` per call (`:136-139`) and hands it to
`scheduling.register` (`:141-147`), which reaches the system scheduler's registration
(`ContinuedTaskScheduling.swift:84-98`). The file's own doc states the constraint that makes this
unbounded: a handler can never be unregistered and the system kills the app on a second
registration of one identifier — which is why the identifier must be UNIQUE. It does not have to be
FRESH, and the current code conflates the two. A session ends at every queue drain, every
expiration and every `.unavailable`, and the next qualifying tap starts a new one, so a single app
run with a dozen download bursts registers a dozen permanent handlers, each retaining a closure and
an identifier string, each live for a launch that can no longer be adopted.

**The two arms differ, and the difference is the whole shape of the fix.** On the
refused-registration arm (`:148-152`) the scheduler stored no handler, so nothing permanent
accumulated. On the throwing-submit arm (`:167-184`) the handler WAS registered successfully and
only the submission failed — so on a device that refuses submissions persistently, every retry adds
another permanent handler that no launch will ever adopt. That arm is where the unboundedness
lives, and a remedy that does not distinguish them either leaves the accumulation in place or
breaks the refusal path's ability to try again.

**What makes the reuse legitimate.** `endSession` takes the pending request back (`:292-314`, the
take-back at `:297` and `:306-308`), so between sessions the scheduler holds no request under the
identifier and the same one is free to be submitted again. The stated requirement — one identifier
is never registered twice — is satisfied exactly as well by a process-scoped identifier as by a
per-session one.

**What the reuse costs, and must be dispositioned rather than discovered.** `handleLaunch` and
`adopt` tell this store's own launch from a leftover by comparing the delivered identifier against
`pendingIdentifier` (`:230-263`). Under one identifier that comparison can no longer distinguish a
leftover launch from a live one by STRING, only by whether a session is currently awaiting a task.
Every state that comparison can be in when a launch arrives must be enumerated and its outcome
stated — including the one outcome that changes.

**What the `live` note does and does not establish.** Its device evidence (15-UAT.md test 1,
`result: pass` on physical iOS 26 hardware, pages landing well past the deleted grace window) proves
post-launch registration is HONOURED, and that stays true and stays written. Its asymmetry argument
— that moving registration earlier is structurally impossible under a per-session identifier — is a
consequence of the per-session choice rather than a defence of it, and cannot survive the choice it
describes.

Purpose: the seam's live implementation stops accumulating an unbounded permanent resource, and the
rule it actually has to satisfy is written where the next reader will look (SC4's seam; SC1's
background coverage rides the launch handler this bounds).
Output: one registered identifier per process re-submitted per session, both failure arms
dispositioned separately, the launch-discrimination consequence enumerated in writing, five
freshness assertions re-derived to the properties they guard, a rewritten live note, and a new
sequential-sessions regression observed RED first.
</objective>
