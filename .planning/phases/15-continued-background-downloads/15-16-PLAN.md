<objective>
Dispose the one prohibition 15-VERIFICATION.md leaves to human judgment, and hand the phase's
device-only checks to the owner.

`BackgroundProcessingClientKey` and `DependencyValues.backgroundProcessingClient` have no source
consumer outside their own declarations: `DownloadClient.live` injects `BackgroundProcessingClient.live`
straight into the coordinator, exactly as D-05 describes, so the registration exists only to host an
unimplemented test value. The phase forbids leaving orphaned or unreferenced background-execution
machinery in the tree, and earlier plans deliberately left this owner-pending rather than guessing.
That is the right call to have made and the wrong state to ship in.

This plan does not decide it. It presents the three dispositions with their concrete shapes and
costs, blocks on the owner, and then implements exactly what was chosen.

Purpose: the prohibition is a judgment about intent, and the two defensible answers point in
opposite directions — one makes the accessor real, the other makes it unnecessary. An executor
picking either one would be inventing an architectural decision, which is precisely what the
flagged-unverified disposition exists to prevent.

Output: a recorded decision, the code change it implies, and the phase's device verification script
restated for the owner's end-of-phase run.
</objective>
