## Owner decisions: deterministic Home card colors (2026-10-08)

17-04 carried a finding to this plan: the focused Home card's dark gradient differed between two
launches of the same capture, which this plan's byte-identical cold-rerun gate cannot accept. At
the owner's request the agent tested the proposed fix before any decision
(`.capture/17-05-imagecolors-ties-01/`):

- `ImageColors` ranked colors by pixel count alone, so equal counts kept the counting
  dictionary's order, which changes with every process. Every fixture image gave 5 to 10
  different results in 10 processes, sometimes swapping a light and a dark background. Ranking
  equal counts by packed color value gives one result per image.
- The palette alone was not enough: ColorfulX's default director places the gradient's points at
  random positions each time the gradient appears, even when Reduce Motion freezes their motion.
  With fixed positions in an experiment, the remaining differences were at most one level, from
  a color transition still running when the screenshot was taken.

The owner approved both changes, each the recommended option:

1. Commit the `ImageColors` tie-break: equally common colors rank by packed value, lowest first.
2. Under Reduce Motion, the card gradient's points take a fixed arrangement. Without Reduce
   Motion they keep their random placement and movement. The capture environment runs with
   Reduce Motion on.

The color transition that runs after the palette arrives is part of this plan's readiness and
settle work.

## Owner decision: Reduce Motion applies a late palette at once (2026-10-08)

Under Reduce Motion the card gradient's first paint is meant to be final, and it is when the
palette is already known. On a first appearance the palette arrives after the card, and ColorfulX
then faded it in over about 4 s, which also left captures one level apart. The owner chose the
recommended option: under Reduce Motion a palette applies at once whenever it arrives, while
without Reduce Motion it still fades in.

This gates a color change, which Phase 16's D-29 keeps ungated. The owner approved it as a D-29
exception for this card (recommended option): the card already skipped its first fade under Reduce
Motion, and a late palette now follows the same rule. The Reduce Motion inventory test records the
exception.

## Owner decision: the reader fixture follows the reader's requests (2026-10-08)

The first reading runs on both devices failed closed: the reader on page 4 requested reader pages
1, 2 and 6 to 20, which the 17-03 fixture did not hold. The app resolves a batch's image URLs
together, from every reader page the batch's preview page lists, and fails them all when one
fails; each page it builds also prefetches a window around it. The 17-03 derivation had assumed
the reader requests only the declared pages. The owner chose the recommended fix: the
acquisition (`Scripts/visual/fixtures.py`, outside this plan's file list) derives the preview

the image of each declared page and its prefetch window at the default preload limit of 10. The
same selection is acquired again anonymously into a new fixture version, and the isolation test
reruns against it.

## Owner decisions: the paging reader and the reader cache (2026-10-08)

With the new fixture the iPad reader showed page 4, but two problems remained. The owner chose
the recommended option for each:

1. **Reader layout.** The default vertical reader settled at a timing-dependent offset: in 4 of
   6 attempts page 4 sat about 330 pt low with page 3's bottom showing, and in 2 it filled the
   screen. Unloaded pages take a 7:10 placeholder (these pages are 1280 by 1791), and the reader
   centers page 4 before the rows above it are measured. Capture scenes therefore run the paging
   reader (`readingDirection = .leftToRight`) in their in-memory settings, so nobody's stored
   settings change. An uncommitted experiment gave two ready, byte-identical iPad runs with
   page 4 framed and no refused request, so the selection's `pages` stay 3 to 5. The vertical
   resume offset is a production defect, filed as a separate task outside 17-05.
2. **Reader cache.** The reader keeps its pages in its own `DataCache`, whose queries are
   asynchronous, while the diagnostics are computed synchronously on every accessibility read,
   so every reader image read as not cached. `DataCache` gains a nonisolated
   `containsData(forKey:)` that checks for the key's file, and `LibraryClient.live.isCached` also
   consults the reader cache, as its clear-all and cache-size functions already do. No
   production code reads `isCached`, so the app behaves as before. Both modules are outside this
   plan's file list.

## Owner decisions: native chrome and the visual simulators (2026-10-08)

T2's status bar override and its device runs raised four questions:

1. **iPad status bar date.** `simctl status_bar override --time 9:41` sets the time but keeps the
   real date, which iPad's status bar shows beside it. simctl freezes the date only for a full
   ISO time, which it reads in the host's time zone like the bar itself, and it derives the
   weekday as if the year were 2000. The owner chose 2026-01-01 at 9:41, so iPad's bar reads
   "9:41 Sat Jan 1" (2026-01-01 is a Thursday). `capture-native.py` passes
   `2026-01-01T09:41:00.000` with the host's offset, so the bar reads 9:41 on any host.
2. **Dedicated simulators.** Another local session created its own iOS 27.0 iPhone Air and
   iPad Pro 11-inch (M5) simulators. The validator resolves each model on the runtime to exactly
   one available simulator, so it found two of each and would refuse every run. At the owner's
   request the agent created two simulators for visual runs, "EhPanda Visual iPhone Air" and
   "EhPanda Visual iPad Pro 11-inch (M5)", set them up per the README's settings table and left
   both Shutdown. The owner chose the recommended design: `environment.resolve_destinations`
   requires exactly one available simulator with that name, model and runtime, and ignores
   others of the same model. The names live in `environment.py`, outside the tracked pin, which
   is unchanged. `environment.py`, its tests and the README are 17-02 files outside this plan's
   file list. The former pinned pair stays as it was.
3. **A settle launch before the captures.** The first iPad gate failed: its two cold launches
   differed only in the status bar's Wi-Fi glyph, one pixel apart (`.capture/17-05-t2-native-02/`).
   A probe (`.capture/17-05-t2-wifi-probe-01/`) showed that the first app the system shows after a
   boot, whichever app and however long after the override, draws that glyph half a point left of
   where every later app draws it; the data network indicator and the cellular item make no
   difference. Gate launch 1 was that first app. The owner chose the recommended option, tested
   first in an uncommitted experiment (`.capture/17-05-t2-experiment-01/`): after setting the
   status bar, the runner opens Settings, waits until two screenshots are identical and quits it,
   so no capture launch is the first app after boot. The experiment's two launches recorded the
   same bytes as the failed run's second launch. Each capture still takes two cold app launches,
   and both families get the same settle launch.
4. **The frame source (2026-10-09).** The final iPad gate failed
   (`.capture/17-05-t2-native-05/`): its first cold launch recorded a framebuffer whose "Other"
   card glass rim differed from the steady frame by one level in 10 pixels, while the test's own
   `XCUIScreen` screenshot of that launch, taken within a second of the runner's frames, matched
   the steady frame. `simctl io screenshot` reads the display framebuffer, which keeps Liquid
   Glass as the display last composed it; the system screenshot renders the screen whole. A probe
   (`.capture/17-05-t2-glass-probe-01/`) and every earlier run found one framebuffer variant in 17
   iPad cold launches, and one system screenshot result in all 17. The plan's first try,
   `simctl io screenshot`, therefore cannot guarantee identical cold reruns, and the stop rule
   applied. The owner chose the recommended option, tested first in an uncommitted experiment
   (`.capture/17-05-t2-xcuiscreen-01/`, `-02/`): the test settles
   `XCUIScreen.main.screenshot()` until two renders a second apart are byte-identical and
   attaches that PNG, and `capture-native.py` takes it from the result bundle as the launch's
   frame, so the handshake is gone. Both experiment runs recorded identical cold reruns, with the
   same bytes as every earlier system screenshot of their coordinate. The frame is 8-bit RGB
   with the overridden status bar and no capsule, and differs from the framebuffer only by a
   level on some glass pixels. No crop, paint, stitch or fake UI is involved; the status bar
   override, the Settings settle launch and every restore are unchanged.
