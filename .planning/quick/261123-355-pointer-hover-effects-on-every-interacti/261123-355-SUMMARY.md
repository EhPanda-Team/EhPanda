## 3. Measured margins and the row technique

Probe: a scratch test (never committed) hosted a `Form`, a `List`, an `.insetGrouped` list, an editing `List` and a sidebar `List` in a real window on the two plan simulators (iPhone Air in portrait, iPad Pro 11-inch (M5) in a 1210 pt wide window) at extra-small, large and accessibility-5 sizes (plan 05; `probe.txt`, `probe-2.txt`).

Switch track: 61 x 28 pt at the trailing edge, centred vertically; rule W grows it 6 pt on every side (`SwitchTrackHoverShape`), applied once inside `AppToggle` for all 25 switches.

Sidebar outcome from `probe-outcome.txt`: `S/R`. The Library list is drawn with the sidebar fill (`.sidebarSelection`, radius 26, the whole cell) while its width is narrower than `store.viewport.width`, and as cards (`.cardRow`) otherwise. In the collapsed (iPhone) sidebar the rest test picked `.cardRow` over a variant with the disclosure margin: the helper with `.cardRow` equalled the baseline in pixels at all three sizes. The reason: no `.cardRowDisclosure` fill was needed.

**The card side margin is not one value.** Two pieces of evidence:
1. The probe read 20 pt on the iPhone in portrait and on the iPad in a 1210 pt window, at all three sizes.
2. The first implementation of plan 05, which cleared the row insets and padded by those measured 20 and 15, moved the Reading rows' content 4 pt inward on both sides on the iPhone in landscape (label at x=104 against 100 for the row without the helper), and the same 4 pt was read in the iPad settings sheet in portrait (x=163 against 159). The margin in those windows reads as 16 pt (20 minus 4) off the pictures.

The cause of the difference, what the system derives the margin from, is not established, and narrow iPad windows are unmeasured.

Why the helper therefore grows and shrinks: a helper that clears the system's margins has to reproduce them in every window, and no value measured here holds in all of them. `cellRowHover` instead grows its view by a fixed amount per fill (card 12 on every side; editing 12 top and bottom, 8 leading, 0 trailing; sidebar 16 leading and trailing, 11 top and bottom), carries the hover there, and takes the same amount back with negative padding. Its layout size is then the content's own size in any window, it sets no row insets, and it reads `defaultMinListRowHeight` for the minimum height (the short row's content height equalled it at every size measured, except the iPhone collapsed sidebar at AX XXXL, 74.67 against 59, which is taller than the minimum and so unaffected).
